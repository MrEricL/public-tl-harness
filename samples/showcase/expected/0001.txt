Chapter 1: The Gate in the Rain

Night rain fell over the Azure Cloud Sect's stone steps, tapping the bronze lamps like countless needles. Shen Yan stood alone at the outer gate, listening to the wind pass through the pines, and drew his cloak tighter around his shoulders. The clouds hung low tonight, yet a thin, untimely silver line lit the distant sky.

Just after midnight, a girl in a straw rain cape climbed the steps. She said she came from Mirror Lake Town and placed a cracked bronze token in Shen Yan's hand. The water in town, she said, had reflected a second moon, and every fishing lamp had gone dark in its reflection. She had also seen an unfamiliar shadow cross the middle of the lake and walk toward the Azure Cloud Sect.

Shen Yan took her to the gate elder. The elder did not question her story. He drew an old map from his sleeve and pointed to the abandoned Stargazing Terrace behind the mountain. A lost sect footwork was sealed there, and it could be used only when starlight and the sound of rain appeared together. The elder said the Starstream Step was not a shortcut for chasing an enemy; it let the world decide where each foot should land.

The alarm suddenly rang three times, and the stone beasts outside the gate opened their blue-green eyes. Shen Yan left the girl beneath the lamp and charged into the rain with his sword drawn. The shadow changed direction seven times among the trees, moving as if it knew every stone. Shen Yan did not pursue it. He listened to the rhythm of the drops striking the ground and followed the stars on the map, stepping left, back, and left again.

Those three steps carried him through tree shadows that had held no gap a moment before, placing him between the shadow and the Stargazing Terrace. A black needle flew from the stranger's sleeve. Shen Yan knocked it down with the spine of his sword and saw the seal of Mirror Lake Town's guild carved into its tail. The shadow leaped into the mist while he hesitated. The elder arrived and pocketed the token. The real answer, he said, lay beneath the lake, and they had to leave before dawn.
